### Q1. In the simplified classical Starling model, interstitial hydrostatic pressure rises while capillary pressure and oncotic gradients are unchanged. Which effect on net outward filtration is expected?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Edema and microvascular fluid exchange | **Exam focus:** NORCET-style original

- **A.** Outward filtration must increase
- **B.** Plasma protein synthesis immediately stops
- **C.** The lymphatic system becomes anatomically absent
- **D.** Outward filtration tends to decrease

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. Outward filtration tends to decrease**

- **A.** Increased interstitial pressure outward gradient oppose karti hai. Yeh capillary hydrostatic pressure increase se different hai.
- **B.** Starling forces fluid movement describe karti hain. Interstitial pressure alone hepatic protein synthesis stop nahi karti.
- **C.** Pressure change lymph drainage affect kar sakta hai, lymphatic structures disappear nahi hote.
- **D.** Interstitial pressure capillary se bahar fluid movement oppose karti hai. Other forces constant hon toh increased tissue pressure net filtration kam kar sakti hai.

</details>

### Q2. A microvascular barrier becomes less able to retain albumin. In a simplified Starling equation, the protein reflection coefficient falls. What does this indicate?

- **B.** The barrier becomes less effective at opposing protein movement
- **C.** Every circulating protein is newly synthesized
- **D.** The arterial blood pressure becomes zero by definition

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. The barrier becomes less effective at opposing protein movement**

- **A.** Lower protein reflection protein permeability se related hai. Water impermeability infer nahi hoti.
- **B.** Reflection coefficient protein ko barrier kitna retain karta hai yeh represent karta hai. Lower coefficient oncotic gradient ke effective fluid-retaining action ko reduce kar sakta hai.
- **C.** Coefficient barrier property hai, liver protein production rate nahi.
- **D.** Reflection coefficient microvascular protein selectivity batata hai. Systemic pressure zero hona iska definition nahi hai.

</details>

### Q4. Under ordinary steady-state conditions in many systemic tissues, how does most excess filtered interstitial fluid return to the blood circulation?

- **C.** By conversion of interstitial water into red blood cells

**Correct: A. Through lymphatic drainage**

- **A.** Revised Starling understanding mein sustained venular reabsorption many tissues mein limited hoti hai. Lymphatics filtered fluid aur proteins ko venous circulation tak return karte hain.
- **B.** Classical teaching universal sustained venular reabsorption assume karti thi. Actual exchange tissue aur physiological state par depend karta hai.
- **C.** Water erythrocytes mein convert nahi hota. Fluid return ke vascular aur lymphatic pathways hote hain.
- **D.** Skin surface evaporation filtered tissue fluid ka main return route nahi. Lymphatic drainage relevant hai.

</details>

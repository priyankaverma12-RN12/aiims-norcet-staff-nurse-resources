## Part 1

### Q1. What is the main effect of an enzyme on the activation energy of a reaction?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Enzymes and biomarkers | **Exam focus:** NORCET-style original

- **A.** It lowers the activation energy
- **B.** It raises the energy difference between products and reactants
- **C.** It changes the equilibrium constant
- **D.** It is consumed stoichiometrically with the substrate

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: A. It lowers the activation energy**

- **A.** Enzyme reaction ka alternate lower-energy pathway deta hai, jisse reaction jaldi hoti hai.
- **B.** Reactants aur products ka overall energy difference enzyme se nahi badalta.
- **C.** Enzyme equilibrium tak pahunchne ki speed badhata hai, equilibrium constant nahi badalta.
- **D.** Enzyme catalytic cycle ke baad generally regenerate ho jaata hai.

</details>

### Q2. In a simple Michaelis-Menten reaction, what does Km represent?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Enzymes and biomarkers | **Exam focus:** NORCET-style original

- **A.** Enzyme concentration at maximum velocity
- **B.** Time needed for complete substrate consumption
- **C.** Substrate concentration at half of Vmax
- **D.** Product concentration at equilibrium

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: C. Substrate concentration at half of Vmax**

- **A.** Enzyme concentration total catalytic capacity ko affect karti hai, Km ki definition nahi hai.
- **B.** Reaction duration Km ka measurement nahi hai.
- **C.** Km woh substrate concentration hai jahan initial rate Vmax ka half hota hai. Yeh kinetics ka parameter hai.
- **D.** Equilibrium product concentration alag thermodynamic property hai.

</details>

### Q3. An inhibitor raises apparent Km while leaving Vmax unchanged. Which idealized inhibition pattern fits?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Enzymes and biomarkers | **Exam focus:** NORCET-style original

- **A.** Pure noncompetitive inhibition
- **B.** Competitive inhibition
- **C.** Uncompetitive inhibition
- **D.** Irreversible destruction of all enzyme molecules

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Competitive inhibition**

- **A.** Pure noncompetitive inhibition mein Vmax kam hota hai aur Km unchanged rehta hai.
- **B.** Competitive inhibitor free enzyme ke liye substrate se compete karta hai. High substrate concentration par maximal rate recover ho sakta hai.
- **C.** Uncompetitive inhibition mein dono apparent parameters kam hote hain.
- **D.** Enzyme destroy hone se available catalytic capacity kam ho jaati hai.

</details>

### Q4. A pure noncompetitive inhibitor binds equally to free enzyme and enzyme-substrate complex. What happens to kinetic parameters?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Enzymes and biomarkers | **Exam focus:** NORCET-style original

- **A.** Vmax increases and Km decreases
- **B.** Both Vmax and Km increase
- **C.** Km increases while Vmax stays unchanged
- **D.** Vmax decreases while Km is unchanged

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. Vmax decreases while Km is unchanged**

- **A.** Catalytic inhibition Vmax increase nahi karti.
- **B.** Dono parameters increase hona is model ka expected pattern nahi hai.
- **C.** Yeh competitive inhibition ka typical pattern hai.
- **D.** Pure noncompetitive binding catalytic capacity kam karti hai, lekin ideal model mein substrate affinity-related Km unchanged rehta hai.

</details>

### Q5. An enzyme assay is performed at saturating substrate. If active enzyme concentration doubles under otherwise identical conditions, what should happen to Vmax?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Enzymes and biomarkers | **Exam focus:** NORCET-style original

- **A.** It becomes zero
- **B.** It remains constant in all cases
- **C.** It approximately doubles
- **D.** It decreases by half

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: C. It approximately doubles**

- **A.** Active enzyme badhne se rate zero nahi hona chahiye.
- **B.** Vmax enzyme amount se independent nahi hai.
- **C.** Saturating substrate par total rate active enzyme molecules aur turnover par depend karta hai. Active enzyme double ho toh Vmax bhi double ho sakta hai.
- **D.** Same catalytic conditions mein enzyme amount badhne se Vmax half nahi hota.

</details>

### Q6. A laboratory warms an enzyme sample far above its normal working temperature and activity falls permanently. What is the most likely explanation?

- **B.** Denaturation of the enzyme protein

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Denaturation of the enzyme protein**

- **A.** Denaturation se functional sites generally kam hote hain.
- **B.** Excessive heat protein folding disturb kar sakti hai aur active site ka shape kharab hone se catalytic activity lose hoti hai.
- **C.** Cell-free assay mein enzyme synthesis automatically nahi badhti.
- **D.** Substrate cofactor mein convert hona heat denaturation ka explanation nahi hai.

</details>

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

### Q12. A patient with sepsis has a rise in cardiac troponin but no demonstrated evidence of myocardial ischemia. Which interpretation is best supported?

- **B.** Normal cardiac muscle despite the elevation

- **D.** Acute myocardial injury, not automatically myocardial infarction

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. Acute myocardial injury, not automatically myocardial infarction**

- **D.** Troponin rise acute myocardial injury suggest karta hai. MI ke liye ischemia ka supporting evidence bhi chahiye.

</details>

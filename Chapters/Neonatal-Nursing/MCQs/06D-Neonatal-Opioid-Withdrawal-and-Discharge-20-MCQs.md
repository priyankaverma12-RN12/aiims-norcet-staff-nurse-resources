### Q01. Which term specifically describes withdrawal caused by prenatal opioid exposure in a newborn?

**Difficulty:** 🟢 Easy · **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH YIELD · Original practice

- **A.** Neonatal respiratory distress syndrome
- **B.** Neonatal hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy
- **C.** Neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome (NOWS)
- **D.** Transient tachypnea of the newborn

<details><summary>✅ Correct answer and detailed explanation</summary>

**Correct answer: C. Neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome (NOWS)**

- **A — Incorrect:** RDS primarily surfactant deficiency-related respiratory disorder hai.
- **B — Incorrect:** HIE hypoxic-ischemic brain dysfunction hai, opioid withdrawal ka term nahi.
- **C — Correct:** NOWS prenatal opioid exposure ke baad withdrawal syndrome ko specifically describe karta hai.
- **D — Incorrect:** TTN delayed fetal lung fluid clearance se related hai.

**Exam clue:** NOWS is opioid-specific withdrawal.

### Q16. A 3.2-kg newborn has a verified prescription for oral morphine 0.05 mg/kg per dose. Pharmacy supplies 0.4 mg/mL. What volume corresponds to the prescribed dose?

**Difficulty:** 🔴 Difficult · **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH YIELD · Original practice

- **A.** 0.04 mL
- **B.** 0.4 mL
- **C.** 4 mL
- **D.** 0.8 mL

<details><summary>✅ Correct answer and detailed explanation</summary>

**Correct answer: B. 0.4 mL**

- **A — Incorrect:** 0.04 mL only 0.016 mg deliver karta hai, one-tenth dose.
- **B — Correct:** 3.2 × 0.05 = 0.16 mg. 0.16 ÷ 0.4 = 0.4 mL. Actual neonatal formulation, concentration and independent check verify karo.
- **C — Incorrect:** 4 mL 1.6 mg deliver karega, tenfold error.
- **D — Incorrect:** 0.8 mL 0.32 mg hai, ordered dose ka double.

**Exam clue:** Calculate mg first; verify concentration and decimal.

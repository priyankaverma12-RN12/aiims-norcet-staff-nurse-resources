### Q01. In subgaleal hemorrhage, blood collects in which anatomical plane?

**Difficulty:** 🟢 Easy · **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH YIELD · Original practice

- **A.** Between the galea aponeurotica and the skull periosteum
- **B.** Within the cerebral ventricles
- **C.** Beneath the retina
- **D.** Inside the middle ear cavity

<details><summary>✅ Correct answer and detailed explanation</summary>

**Correct answer: A. Between the galea aponeurotica and the skull periosteum**

- **A — Correct:** Subgaleal potential space galea aur periosteum ke beech hai. Large space mein blood loss significant ho sakta hai.
- **B — Incorrect:** Ventricular blood IVH hai, extracranial subgaleal bleed nahi.
- **C — Incorrect:** Retinal bleeding eye ke andar hoti hai.
- **D — Incorrect:** Middle ear cavity scalp compartment nahi hai.

**Exam clue:** Subgaleal space is extracranial and extensive.

**Reference:** [Clinical source](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/perinatal-problems/birth-injuries) · [Concept guide](../Concepts/05D-Neonatal-Birth-Trauma-and-Functional-Care.md)

### Q05. What does neuropraxia mean in the classification of neonatal peripheral nerve injury?

**Difficulty:** 🟢 Easy · **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH YIELD · Original practice

- **A.** Complete separation of every nerve root from the spinal cord
- **B.** Temporary nerve conduction block with potential recovery
- **C.** A fracture of the skull alone
- **D.** Permanent absence of the entire arm

<details><summary>✅ Correct answer and detailed explanation</summary>

**Correct answer: B. Temporary nerve conduction block with potential recovery**

- **A — Incorrect:** Root avulsion severe injury hai, temporary conduction block nahi.
- **B — Correct:** Neuropraxia conduction interruption hai without complete nerve disruption. Recovery generally possible hai.
- **C — Incorrect:** Fracture bone injury hai; neuropraxia nerve injury category hai.
- **D — Incorrect:** Limb absence congenital structural finding hai, conduction injury nahi.

**Exam clue:** Neuropraxia is less severe than nerve disruption.

### Q01. At an antenatal visit, a clinically well woman has a resting respiratory rate of 19/min, no respiratory distress and otherwise reassuring observations. Which interpretation is most appropriate?

- **A.** The respiratory rate establishes severe hypoventilation
- **B.** The reading is compatible with normal resting breathing, but clinical context and trends still matter
- **C.** Every healthy pregnant woman must have this exact respiratory rate
- **D.** The reading confirms maternal respiratory alkalosis without an arterial blood gas

<details><summary>✅ Sahi answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct answer: B. The reading is compatible with normal resting breathing, but clinical context and trends still matter**

- **A (Incorrect):** Sirf respiratory rate 19/min se severe hypoventilation diagnose nahi hoti. Breathing depth, oxygenation, symptoms aur carbon dioxide clearance ko bhi assess karte hain.
- **B (Correct):** Reassuring assessment ke saath 19 breaths/min normal resting breathing ke compatible hai. Pregnancy mein minute ventilation zyada tar tidal volume badhne se increase hoti hai. Har woman ki respiratory rate ek narrow fixed interval mein hona zaroori nahi hai.
- **C (Incorrect):** Normal resting rate individual aur clinical condition ke hisab se vary karti hai. Sab healthy women ke liye ek exact value mandatory nahi hoti.
- **D (Incorrect):** Respiratory alkalosis acid-base pattern hai. Sirf breathing rate dekhkar arterial pH aur PaCO₂ establish nahi kar sakte.

**Reported historical source:** [NHM Rajasthan CHO — 10 November 2020, retrieved Testbook question page](https://testbook.com/question-answer/normal-respiration-in-pregnant-women--6322de20c75fd1c9664b90af). The page selects 18–20/min. This is a reported key, not a clinically exclusive pregnancy reference interval. No official key authenticated.

**Clinical references:** [NCBI maternal changes](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539766/), [ERS respiratory physiology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4818213/). New persistent tachypnea or dyspnea needs assessment rather than reassurance.

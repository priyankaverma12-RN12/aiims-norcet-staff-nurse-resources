### Q1. A nonpregnant adult has normal hemoglobin, low serum ferritin and no evidence of active inflammation. What interpretation is most appropriate?

**Difficulty:** 🟡 Medium · **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH YIELD · Reported-exam adaptation

- **A.** Iron deficiency is excluded because hemoglobin is normal.
- **B.** Iron stores may already be depleted before anemia develops.
- **C.** The findings establish vitamin B12 deficiency.
- **D.** Low ferritin alone establishes the site of blood loss.

<details><summary>✅ Correct answer and detailed explanation</summary>

**Correct answer: B. Iron stores may already be depleted before anemia develops.**

- **A (Incorrect):** Hemoglobin iron depletion ke early stage mein normal reh sakta hai. Normal hemoglobin ki wajah se low iron stores exclude nahi karne chahiye.
- **B (Correct):** Ferritin stored iron ka marker hai. Active inflammation ke absence mein low ferritin depleted iron stores ko support karta hai, even before hemoglobin falls. Clinical history aur cause assessment bhi important hain.
- **C (Incorrect):** B12 deficiency ko appropriate B12 testing aur clinical findings se evaluate karte hain. Low ferritin alone B12 deficiency establish nahi karta.
- **D (Incorrect):** Ferritin iron stores ke baare mein information deta hai, bleeding ki anatomical site nahi batata. Menstrual, gastrointestinal aur other causes ko relevant evaluation se assess karna hota hai.

**Reported exam evidence:** [HPSSC Staff Nurse, 1 August 2021, reported ferritin-testing question](https://testbook.com/question-answer/which-is-the-best-test-to-detect-iron-deficiency-i--697c5911be3d4db5590cbba6). The source's reported answer was ferritin. This newly written scenario tests the related early-depletion concept rather than reproducing the item. [Post code 892 paper corroboration](https://hpgeneralstudies.com/solved-paper-hp-staff-nurse-exam-2021-part-1-hpssc-hamirpur/) and [third-party key archive lead](https://himexam.com/hpssc-staff-nurse-post-code-892-exam-official-answer-key-2021/) located. Exact original question number and official answer-key mapping remain pending.

**Clinical reference:** [WHO ferritin guideline](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240000124).

### Q2. In antibody-mediated autoimmune hemolytic anemia, which immunological mechanism best describes antibody binding to erythrocyte surface antigens followed by cell injury or clearance?

**Difficulty:** 🟡 Medium · **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH YIELD · Reported-exam adaptation

- **A.** Type I immediate IgE-mediated hypersensitivity
- **B.** Type III soluble immune-complex deposition
- **C.** Type II antibody-mediated hypersensitivity
- **D.** Type IV delayed T-cell-mediated hypersensitivity

<details><summary>✅ Correct answer and detailed explanation</summary>

**Correct answer: C. Type II antibody-mediated hypersensitivity.**

- **A (Incorrect):** Type I reaction mein IgE-mediated mast cell activation important hoti hai, jaise immediate allergic reaction mein. RBC surface-directed antibody injury ka typical classification Type II hai.
- **B (Incorrect):** Type III reaction mein soluble antigen-antibody complexes tissue mein deposit ho kar inflammation produce kar sakte hain. Direct erythrocyte surface antibody binding is mechanism se different hai.
- **C (Correct):** Type II hypersensitivity mein antibodies cell surface ya tissue antigens ko target karti hain. Autoimmune hemolysis mein RBC-bound antibodies clearance ya complement-related injury promote kar sakti hain. Har nonimmune hemolytic anemia ko Type II nahi kehna chahiye.
- **D (Incorrect):** Type IV hypersensitivity T cells se mediated hoti hai aur antibody binding primary mechanism nahi hota. Is scenario mein antibody-directed red cell injury specifically described hai.

**Reported exam evidence:** [DSSSB Nursing Officer, 28 August 2019, Shift 3, reported hypersensitivity question](https://testbook.com/question-answer/identify-the-type-of-hypersensitivity-in-haemolyti--61600260fc2f042cc51daeef). Third-party answer reports cytotoxic hypersensitivity. The adaptation explicitly limits the mechanism to immune hemolysis. Official question number and final key not authenticated.

**Clinical reference:** [Merck Manual: Autoimmune hemolytic anemia](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/hematology-and-oncology/anemias-caused-by-hemolysis/autoimmune-hemolytic-anemia).

### Q1. A pediatric transfusion plan is reviewed because a cellular component would remain outside controlled storage beyond its permitted administration window. Which microbiological concern supports respecting that limit?

**Difficulty:** 🟡 Medium · **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH YIELD · 🔵 SCTIMST Staff Nurse (Temporary) 09 August 2015, Q60 related-concept adaptation (official paper text, key unverified)

- **A.** The recipient will necessarily develop donor T-cell engraftment
- **B.** Prolonged uncontrolled exposure may allow microbial proliferation in a contaminated component
- **C.** Every delay creates an ABO antibody in the recipient immediately
- **D.** The component automatically becomes compatible with all blood groups

<details><summary>✅ Correct answer and detailed explanation</summary>

**Correct answer: B. Prolonged uncontrolled exposure may allow microbial proliferation in a contaminated component**

- **A (Incorrect):** TA-GVHD viable donor lymphocytes se related hai. Administration deadline ka microbial safety rationale donor T-cell engraftment ko specifically prevent karna nahi hai.
- **B (Correct):** Agar component contaminated ho toh uncontrolled exposure microbial growth ka opportunity increase kar sakta hai. Isliye validated handling aur time limits follow karni chahiye. Pediatric dosing aur slow rate ke liye authorized aliquot planning helpful ho sakti hai.
- **C (Incorrect):** ABO antibody formation exposure ke elapsed bedside time se necessarily immediate nahi hoti. Time limits component handling safety se related hain, compatibility testing separate hai.
- **D (Incorrect):** Delay donor red cell antigens ya recipient antibodies remove nahi karta. Compatible product selection aur handling deadline dono separate safety requirements hain.

**Exam clue:** Historical question ka microbial-risk concept retain karo, outdated duration ko universal current practice mat samjho.

**Clinical references:** [Indian standards](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-03/1491_0.pdf) · [Bacterial infection guidance](https://www.lifeblood.com.au/health-professionals/clinical-practice/adverse-events/transfusion-transmitted-bacterial-infection)

## Clinical pattern

### Q1. A patient has active glomerular sediment, edema and increasing creatinine. The urine protein excretion is also in the nephrotic range. Which interpretation is appropriate?

**Difficulty:** 🟡 Medium · **Priority:** 🔴 MOST IMPORTANT

- **A.** Nephrotic-range protein excludes glomerular inflammation
- **B.** Heavy proteinuria establishes isolated tubular injury
- **C.** Nephritic and nephrotic features can coexist
- **D.** The urinary sediment must be a laboratory error

<details><summary>✅ Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct answer: C. Nephritic and nephrotic features can coexist**

**Kyun sahi hai?** Nephritic aur nephrotic patterns ek saath mil sakte hain. Glomerular inflammation RBC leakage cause kar sakti hai aur barrier damage severe protein loss bhi de sakta hai. Cause and severity assess karna zaroori hai.

**Baaki options kyun galat hain?**

- **A.** Heavy proteinuria inflammatory disease ko exclude nahi karti.
- **B.** Glomerular barrier damage heavy proteinuria cause kar sakta hai.
- **D.** Sediment ko automatically error maan kar ignore nahi karna chahiye.

**Exam clue:** Mixed patterns are possible.

### Q3. Why can acute nephritic edema occur in a patient whose serum albumin remains near normal?

**Difficulty:** 🟡 Medium · **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH YIELD

### Q9. A spot urine has total protein 180 mg/dL and creatinine 120 mg/dL. What is the urine protein-to-creatinine ratio expressed in mg/g?

**Difficulty:** 🔴 Difficult · **Priority:** 🔴 MOST IMPORTANT

- **A.** 150 mg/g
- **B.** 1,500 mg/g
- **C.** 667 mg/g
- **D.** 15 mg/g

<details><summary>✅ Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct answer: B. 1,500 mg/g**

**Kyun sahi hai?** 180 ÷ 120 = 1.5 mg/mg. One gram mein 1,000 mg hote hain, so 1.5 × 1,000 = 1,500 mg/g. Ye spot estimate hai, measured 24-hour excretion nahi.

**Baaki options kyun galat hain?**

- **A.** 150 incorrect tenfold scaling hai.
- **C.** 667 ratio reverse karne se approximately aata hai.
- **D.** 15 correct unit conversion nahi hai.

**Exam clue:** Convert mg/mg to mg/g by multiplying by 1,000.

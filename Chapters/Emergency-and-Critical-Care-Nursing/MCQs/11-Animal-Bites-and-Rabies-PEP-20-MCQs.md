### Q01. A man reports that a suspected rabid dog licked his forearm. Examination confirms intact skin, without any wound or mucosal contact. Under Indian NRCP 2019, how should this exposure be classified?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** High | **Type:** Original practice | **Topic:** Animal bites and rabies PEP

- **A.** Category II because saliva touched skin
- **B.** Category I, with no rabies biologicals indicated if the history is reliable
- **C.** Category III because all dog contact is severe
- **D.** Category III because the dog is suspected rabid

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Category I, with no rabies biologicals indicated if the history is reliable**

- **A.** Category II ke liye nibbling ya minor skin abrasion jaisi exposure chahiye. Sirf intact skin contact sufficient nahi.
- **B.** Intact skin rabies virus ke entry ko rokne wali barrier hai. Reliable history mein sirf intact skin lick category I hai.
- **C.** Category III mein transdermal injury ya saliva ka broken skin ya mucosa se contact hota hai. Har dog contact category III nahi.
- **D.** Animal status important hai, lekin exposure category contact ke route aur skin integrity par bhi depend karti hai.

**Exam clue:** Intact skin ki reliable confirmation category I ka key clue hai.

**Reference:** [Indian NRCP 2019 §2.1](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Rabies-Guidelines.pdf).

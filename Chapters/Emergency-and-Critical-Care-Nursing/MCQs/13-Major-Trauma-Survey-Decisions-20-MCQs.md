### Q01. During assessment of major blunt trauma, suspected intra-abdominal bleeding remains a concern despite a negative FAST examination. Which interpretation is safest?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High | **Type:** Original practice | **Topic:** Major trauma survey decisions

- **A.** A negative FAST excludes all clinically significant abdominal bleeding
- **B.** A negative FAST does not exclude intraperitoneal or retroperitoneal haemorrhage
- **C.** FAST directly measures the circulating blood volume
- **D.** The negative result establishes that the patient needs no further assessment

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. A negative FAST does not exclude intraperitoneal or retroperitoneal haemorrhage**

- **A.** Test sensitivity perfect nahi hai. Patient ka mechanism, findings aur hemodynamic trend phir bhi important hain.
- **B.** FAST certain areas mein free fluid identify karta hai, lekin all bleeding reliably exclude nahi karta. Retroperitoneal blood aur early bleeding miss ho sakti hai.
- **C.** Ultrasound free fluid aur selected injuries assess karta hai, circulating volume directly measure nahi karta.
- **D.** Negative imaging result alone discharge ya no further assessment ka basis nahi hai.

**Exam clue:** Negative FAST is not a rule-out test for all traumatic haemorrhage.

**Reference:** [NICE NG39 (UK guidance) 1.5.30](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng39/resources/major-trauma-assessment-and-initial-management-pdf-1837400761285).

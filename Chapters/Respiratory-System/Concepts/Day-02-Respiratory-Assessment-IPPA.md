## Respiratory assessment — Inspection

### First look (dekho)
Inspection means **observe without touching**. Introduce yourself; check patient comfort and position.

Look at:
- **Respiratory rate** (count breaths/min), **rhythm** and **depth**.
- Work of breathing: accessory muscles, nasal flaring, retractions.
- Chest movement: whether both sides rise **symmetrically**.
- Posture and ability to speak in full sentences; visible distress.
- Skin/lip colour and oxygen saturation as part of broader assessment.

**Usual resting adult RR: 12–20/min** (teaching reference). Abnormally increased rate = tachypnea; abnormally decreased rate = bradypnea.

**Important:** A visual observation alone does not establish a disease. Sudden respiratory distress requires urgent clinical assessment according to protocol.

## Respiratory assessment — Palpation

### Touch (haath se feel karna)
Palpation checks:
- **Chest expansion:** compare both sides for symmetric movement.
- **Tracheal position:** usually midline.
- **Tenderness**, lumps, local swelling.
- **Tactile fremitus:** vibration felt on chest wall as patient speaks (e.g., “ninety-nine”).

#### Fremitus memory
- **Increased fremitus** may occur when dense lung tissue transmits vibrations better, such as **consolidation** with a patent bronchus.
- **Decreased fremitus** may occur with pleural effusion, pneumothorax or airway obstruction.

Interpret with all other findings. Palpation does **not** replace imaging or clinical evaluation.

## Respiratory assessment — Percussion

### Tapping to judge underlying tissue
Percussion is tapping the chest wall and **listening to the resulting note**. Compare corresponding sites on both sides.

| Note | Usual significance |
|---|---|
| **Resonant** | Expected over healthy air-filled lung |
| **Dull** | Denser tissue or fluid, e.g., consolidation/effusion |
| **Hyperresonant** | Extra air, e.g., pneumothorax or hyperinflation |
| **Flat** | Very dense structure such as muscle or bone |

**Exam memory:** Normal air = resonant; fluid/solid = dull; extra air = hyperresonant.

Percussion findings support but cannot by themselves confirm a diagnosis.

## Respiratory assessment — Auscultation

### Listening with a stethoscope
Ask for slow, slightly deeper breaths through mouth while listening **side-to-side** over matching chest sites.

#### Normal sounds
- **Vesicular:** soft, low-pitched; heard over most peripheral lung fields.
- **Bronchial:** louder, higher-pitched; normally heard over trachea/manubrium, not peripheral lung.
- **Bronchovesicular:** intermediate; near main bronchi (anterior upper sternum, between scapulae).

#### Adventitious sounds
| Sound | Description | Typical association (not a diagnosis) |
|---|---|---|
| **Wheeze** | Musical, usually high-pitched | Narrowed airways |
| **Crackles (rales)** | Discontinuous popping | Fluid, collapsed/reopening small airways |
| **Rhonchi** | Low-pitched snoring | Secretions/large airway narrowing |
| **Stridor** | Harsh, often inspiratory | **Upper airway obstruction — urgent assessment** |
| **Pleural friction rub** | Grating/creaking | Inflamed pleural surfaces |

**Sequence:** Inspection → Palpation → Percussion → Auscultation (**IPPA**).

## Arterial blood gases — basics & interpretation

#### Q1. ABG pH 7.29, PaCO₂ 53 mmHg, HCO₃⁻ 24 mEq/L. Best primary disorder?

- **A.** Respiratory acidosis
- **B.** Respiratory alkalosis
- **C.** Metabolic acidosis
- **D.** Metabolic alkalosis

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**A.** Low pH + high CO₂ with normal bicarbonate fits acute/uncompensated respiratory acidosis.

</details>

#### Q2. ABG pH 7.49, PaCO₂ 31, HCO₃⁻ 24. Best interpretation?

- **A.** Metabolic acidosis
- **B.** Respiratory alkalosis
- **C.** Respiratory acidosis
- **D.** Metabolic alkalosis

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**B.** High pH and low CO₂ indicate primary respiratory alkalosis.

</details>

#### Q3. ABG pH 7.27, PaCO₂ 40, HCO₃⁻ 17. Primary abnormality?

- **A.** Respiratory alkalosis
- **B.** Respiratory acidosis
- **C.** Metabolic acidosis
- **D.** Metabolic alkalosis

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**C.** Low pH with low HCO₃⁻ suggests metabolic acidosis.

</details>

#### Q4. Which parameter primarily reflects respiratory acid-base component?

- **A.** HCO₃⁻
- **B.** Hemoglobin
- **C.** PaO₂
- **D.** PaCO₂

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**D.** PaCO₂ is respiratory component; HCO₃⁻ metabolic.

</details>

#### Q5. The usual arterial pH teaching interval is:

- **A.** 7.35–7.45
- **B.** 7.25–7.35
- **C.** 7.45–7.55
- **D.** 7.15–7.25

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**A.** Normal arterial pH is approximately 7.35–7.45.

</details>

#### Q6. pH 7.48, PaCO₂ 40, HCO₃⁻ 30 suggests:

- **A.** Metabolic acidosis
- **B.** Metabolic alkalosis
- **C.** Respiratory acidosis
- **D.** Respiratory alkalosis

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**B.** High pH and high HCO₃⁻ support primary metabolic alkalosis.

</details>

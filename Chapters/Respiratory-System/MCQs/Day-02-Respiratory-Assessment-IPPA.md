## Respiratory assessment — Inspection

#### Q1. The first IPPA step in routine respiratory examination is:

- **A.** Percussion
- **B.** Inspection
- **C.** Auscultation
- **D.** Palpation

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**B.** Inspection means observing breathing before touching.

</details>

#### Q2. Which finding primarily belongs to inspection?

- **A.** Chest expansion symmetry
- **B.** Tactile fremitus
- **C.** Percussion note
- **D.** Breath sound

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**A.** Chest movement can be observed visually.

</details>

#### Q3. A resting adult RR of 26/min is:

- **A.** Within 12–20/min
- **B.** Bradypnea
- **C.** Tachypnea
- **D.** Apnea

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**C.** 26 exceeds the typical adult resting range.

</details>

## Respiratory assessment — Palpation

#### Q1. A nurse places hands on the posterior chest to compare movement during inhalation. What is assessed?

- **A.** Chest expansion
- **B.** Percussion note
- **C.** Bronchial breathing
- **D.** PaCO₂

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**A.** Palpation can compare right and left chest expansion.

</details>

#### Q2. A vibration felt over chest while patient speaks is called:

- **A.** Resonance
- **B.** Fremitus
- **C.** Wheeze
- **D.** Stridor

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**B.** Tactile fremitus is palpable vocal vibration.

</details>

#### Q3. Tactile fremitus may increase with:

- **A.** Pleural effusion
- **B.** Pneumothorax
- **C.** Lung consolidation with open bronchus
- **D.** Large airway obstruction

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**C.** Consolidated tissue can transmit sound vibration better.

</details>

#### Q4. A normally positioned trachea is typically:

- **A.** Deviated right
- **B.** Deviated left
- **C.** Not palpable
- **D.** Midline

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**D.** Trachea is normally near the midline.

</details>

## Respiratory assessment — Percussion

#### Q1. Expected percussion sound over normal aerated lung:

- **A.** Dullness
- **B.** Resonance
- **C.** Flatness
- **D.** Tympany

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**B.** Healthy air-filled lung usually sounds resonant.

</details>

#### Q2. Percussion over pleural fluid usually gives:

- **A.** Hyperresonance
- **B.** Tympany
- **C.** Dullness
- **D.** Normal resonance

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**C.** Pleural fluid produces a relatively dull note.

</details>

#### Q3. A hyperresonant percussion note suggests:

- **A.** Extra air
- **B.** Lung consolidation
- **C.** Pleural effusion
- **D.** Bone

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**A.** More air can yield hyperresonance.

</details>

#### Q4. Which method compares percussion notes side to side?

- **A.** Only one apex tested
- **B.** Listen only at sternum
- **C.** Test over clothing
- **D.** Compare corresponding areas

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**D.** Bilateral comparison helps detect asymmetry.

</details>

## Respiratory assessment — Auscultation

#### Q1. Normal breath sound over most peripheral lung fields is:

- **A.** Bronchial
- **B.** Vesicular
- **C.** Stridor
- **D.** Pleural rub

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**B.** Vesicular breathing predominates over peripheral lung.

</details>

#### Q2. High-pitched musical continuous breath sound is:

- **A.** Wheeze
- **B.** Crackles
- **C.** Friction rub
- **D.** Absent breath sound

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**A.** Wheezing often reflects narrowed airways.

</details>

#### Q3. Discontinuous popping on lung auscultation is called:

- **A.** Stridor
- **B.** Rhonchi
- **C.** Crackles
- **D.** Bronchial breathing

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**C.** Crackles are intermittent pops.

</details>

#### Q4. A harsh inspiratory sound at the neck suggesting upper-airway obstruction is:

- **A.** Rhonchi
- **B.** Vesicular
- **C.** Crackles
- **D.** Stridor

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**D.** Stridor can signal upper airway compromise and deserves urgent assessment.

</details>

#### Q5. Grating sound associated with inflamed pleural surfaces:

- **A.** Pleural friction rub
- **B.** Wheeze
- **C.** Bronchovesicular
- **D.** Rhonchi

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**A.** A pleural friction rub comes from irritated pleural surfaces.

</details>

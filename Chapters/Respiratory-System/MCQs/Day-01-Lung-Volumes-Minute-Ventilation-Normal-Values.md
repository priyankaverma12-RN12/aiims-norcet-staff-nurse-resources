## Lung volumes & capacities

#### Q1. What remains after maximal forced expiration?

- **A.** TV
- **B.** IRV
- **C.** RV
- **D.** ERV

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**C.** Residual volume cannot be voluntarily expelled.

</details>

#### Q2. What remains after a normal quiet expiration?

- **A.** VC
- **B.** FRC
- **C.** IRV
- **D.** IC

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**B.** Functional residual capacity equals ERV + RV.

</details>

#### Q3. If IC = 3450 mL and TV = 500 mL, IRV equals:

- **A.** 3950
- **B.** 2950
- **C.** 2450
- **D.** 3450

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**B.** IRV = IC − TV = 3450 − 500 = 2950 mL.

</details>

#### Q4. If VC = 4400 mL, ERV = 900 mL and TV = 500 mL, IRV equals:

- **A.** 3000
- **B.** 3400
- **C.** 3900
- **D.** 2500

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**A.** IRV = VC − ERV − TV = 4400 − 900 − 500 = 3000 mL.

</details>

#### Q5. If TLC = 5900 mL and IC = 3600 mL, FRC equals:

- **A.** 1300
- **B.** 3600
- **C.** 2300
- **D.** 9500

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**C.** FRC = TLC − IC = 2300 mL.

</details>

## Minute ventilation (numericals)

#### Q1. RR 17/min and TV 400 mL. MV is:

- **A.** 6.8 L/min
- **B.** 7.2 L/min
- **C.** 5.8 L/min
- **D.** 8.0 L/min

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**A.** 17 × 400 = 6800 mL/min = 6.8 L/min.

</details>

#### Q2. MV is 8.1 L/min and TV is 450 mL. RR is:

- **A.** 15/min
- **B.** 16/min
- **C.** 18/min
- **D.** 20/min

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**C.** 8100 ÷ 450 = 18 breaths/min.

</details>

#### Q3. MV is 7.2 L/min and RR is 16/min. TV is:

- **A.** 350 mL
- **B.** 400 mL
- **C.** 450 mL
- **D.** 500 mL

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**C.** 7200 ÷ 16 = 450 mL.

</details>

#### Q4. Before: RR 10, TV 600; after: RR 15, TV 400. Change in MV?

- **A.** Increase by 2 L/min
- **B.** Decrease by 1 L/min
- **C.** Increase by 1 L/min
- **D.** No change

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**D.** Both are 6000 mL/min = 6 L/min.

</details>

#### Q5. MV rises from 6 L/min to 7.5 L/min. Percent increase?

- **A.** 15%
- **B.** 20%
- **C.** 25%
- **D.** 30%

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**C.** (7.5−6)/6 × 100 = 25%.

</details>

## Normal adult respiratory values

#### Q1. Which value is outside usual normal arterial pH range?

- **A.** 7.38
- **B.** 7.42
- **C.** 7.46
- **D.** 7.40

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**C.** Typical arterial pH is 7.35–7.45.

</details>

#### Q2. Which is a normal resting adult RR?

- **A.** 9/min
- **B.** 16/min
- **C.** 24/min
- **D.** 28/min

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**B.** Standard adult resting exam range is about 12–20/min.

</details>

#### Q3. Which value is within usual PaCO₂ reference range?

- **A.** 31 mmHg
- **B.** 48 mmHg
- **C.** 52 mmHg
- **D.** 39 mmHg

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**D.** Common PaCO₂ range is 35–45 mmHg.

</details>

#### Q4. For healthy adults near sea level, which SpO₂ is within usual exam range?

- **A.** 87%
- **B.** 91%
- **C.** 93%
- **D.** 98%

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**D.** SpO₂ of 98% is within commonly taught 95–100%.

</details>

#### Q5. SpO₂ 97% means PaO₂ must be exactly 97 mmHg.

- **A.** True
- **B.** False
- **C.** Always if breathing normally
- **D.** Only after exercise

<details><summary>✅ Answer & explanation</summary>

**B.** SpO₂ is saturation %, while PaO₂ is partial pressure mmHg; they are not numerically interchangeable.

</details>

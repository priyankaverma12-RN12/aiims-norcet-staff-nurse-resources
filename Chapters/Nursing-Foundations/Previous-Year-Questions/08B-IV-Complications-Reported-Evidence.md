## 🔵 AIIMS Bhopal Nursing Officer 23 May 2018 Shift II — third-party reported question

### Q1. A historical question associates slow infusion, redness and tenderness with thrombophlebitis. In a modern clinical review, the nurse observes those findings but has no evidence confirming a thrombus. Which documentation is most defensible?

**Difficulty:** 🟡 Medium · **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH YIELD · Source-based interpretation

A. Suspected peripheral phlebitis with observed signs, with further evaluation for thrombus as indicated
B. Confirmed deep vein thrombosis based solely on the infusion rate
C. Confirmed bloodstream infection without systemic assessment
D. Normal functioning peripheral access with no need for review

<details>
<summary>✅ Sahi answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct answer: A. Suspected peripheral phlebitis with observed signs, with further evaluation for thrombus as indicated**

- **A (Correct):** Redness, tenderness aur slow flow inflamed ya malfunctioning vein access ka clue de sakte hain. Thrombophlebitis mein thrombus bhi hota hai. Observed signs ko accurately record karo aur indicated evaluation se diagnosis clarify karo. Affected catheter ko use karte rehna safe nahi hai.
- **B (Incorrect):** Slow flow se deep vein thrombosis establish nahi hota. Site assessment aur appropriate diagnostic evaluation chahiye.
- **C (Incorrect):** Local inflammation mechanical, chemical ya infection-related ho sakti hai. Bloodstream infection ko bina assessment confirm nahi karna chahiye.
- **D (Incorrect):** Redness aur tenderness normal working site ki expected findings nahi hain. Timely review aur appropriate action zaroori hai.

**Exam clue:** Historical option mein thrombophlebitis best available choice ho sakta hai. Modern documentation mein observed signs aur confirmed diagnosis ko alag rakho.

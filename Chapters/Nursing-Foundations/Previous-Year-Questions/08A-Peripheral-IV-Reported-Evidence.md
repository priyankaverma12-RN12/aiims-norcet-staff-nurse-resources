### Q1. A training stock audit finds a peripheral cannula with a grey hub inside packaging labelled 18G. Under conventional colour coding, what is the best interpretation before clinical use?

**Difficulty:** 🟡 Medium · **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH YIELD · Source-based adaptation

A. Grey conventionally indicates 16G, so isolate the discrepant device and verify its identity.
B. Grey conventionally indicates 18G, so the discrepancy can be ignored.
C. Grey indicates 24G, so use it immediately for a neonate.
D. Hub colour is sufficient to override a contradictory manufacturer label.

<details>
<summary>✅ Sahi answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct answer: A. Grey conventionally indicates 16G, so isolate the discrepant device and verify its identity.**

- **A (Correct):** Standard exam colour scheme mein grey 16G se associated hai aur green 18G se. Actual package mismatch mein device ko use karne se pehle manufacturer details aur stock policy se verify karna chahiye.
- **B (Incorrect):** Conventional 18G colour green hai. Grey hub aur 18G package ko bina check accept karna safe nahi hai.
- **C (Incorrect):** Conventional 24G colour yellow hai. Neonate ka gauge patient assessment aur therapy se choose hota hai, sirf colour se nahi.
- **D (Incorrect):** Colour useful quick clue hai, lekin packaging aur device identification mismatch ko override nahi karta.

**Exam clue:** Colour scheme exam mein useful hai, clinical practice mein verified gauge aur manufacturer label zaroor check karo.

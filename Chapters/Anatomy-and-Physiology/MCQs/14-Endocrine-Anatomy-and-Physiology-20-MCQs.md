### Q1. Which brain region releases regulatory hormones into the hypophyseal portal circulation?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** Medium

- **A.** Hypothalamus
- **B.** Cerebellum
- **C.** Medulla as the principal source
- **D.** Pineal gland

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: A. Hypothalamus**

Hypothalamus releasing aur inhibiting hormones banata hai jo portal vessels se anterior pituitary tak pahunchte hain. Isse pituitary secretion regulate hoti hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Cerebellum movement coordinate karta hai. Medulla vital reflexes mein hai. Pineal gland melatonin banati hai.

</details>

### Q2. Where are ADH and oxytocin synthesized before their release from the posterior pituitary?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** Posterior pituitary secretory epithelial cells
- **B.** Thyroid follicular cells
- **C.** Adrenal cortex
- **D.** Hypothalamic neurons

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. Hypothalamic neurons**

ADH aur oxytocin hypothalamic neurons mein bante hain. Axons ke through posterior pituitary tak transport hote hain aur wahan se blood mein release hote hain.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Posterior pituitary main synthesis site nahi hai. Thyroid thyroid hormones banati hai. Adrenal cortex steroid hormones banati hai.

</details>

### Q4. A healthy person has increased circulating free T4. What feedback response is expected?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** Increased ACTH as the primary thyroid feedback response
- **B.** Reduced hypothalamic TRH and pituitary TSH secretion
- **C.** Increased TSH without limit
- **D.** Complete cessation of all pituitary hormones

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Reduced hypothalamic TRH and pituitary TSH secretion**

Thyroid hormone high ho toh hypothalamus aur pituitary ko negative feedback milta hai. TRH aur TSH kam ho sakte hain, jisse thyroid stimulation reduce hoti hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** TSH usually high nahi hota. Other pituitary axes completely stop nahi hoti. ACTH adrenal axis regulate karta hai.

</details>

### Q5. Which thyroid cells secrete calcitonin?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** Medium

- **A.** Adrenal chromaffin cells
- **B.** Parafollicular C cells
- **C.** Follicular cells
- **D.** Parathyroid chief cells

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Parafollicular C cells**

Thyroid ke parafollicular C cells calcitonin secrete karti hain. Ye bone resorption ko reduce karne mein role rakh sakta hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Follicular cells T3/T4 banati hain. Chief cells PTH banati hain. Chromaffin cells catecholamines release karti hain.

</details>

### Q6. Which effect is expected when PTH rises in response to low serum calcium?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** Increased renal calcium conservation and increased phosphate excretion
- **B.** Increased renal calcium loss and phosphate retention as the main response
- **C.** Complete inhibition of calcitriol production
- **D.** Permanent closure of all calcium channels

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: A. Increased renal calcium conservation and increased phosphate excretion**

PTH kidney mein calcium reabsorption badhata hai aur phosphate reabsorption kam karta hai. Active vitamin D formation bhi stimulate kar sakta hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Calcium conserve hota hai. Phosphate excretion badhti hai. Calcitriol production inhibit nahi hoti. Calcium channels permanently close nahi hote.

</details>

### Q7. Which pancreatic islet cells produce insulin?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** Medium

- **A.** Alpha cells
- **B.** Delta cells
- **C.** Acinar cells
- **D.** Beta cells

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. Beta cells**

Beta cells insulin release karti hain jab glucose badhta hai. Insulin muscle aur fat cells ko glucose uptake mein help karti hai aur liver glucose output kam kar sakti hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Alpha cells glucagon banati hain. Delta cells somatostatin banati hain. Acinar cells digestive enzymes banati hain.

</details>

### Q8. During fasting, which action of glucagon helps maintain blood glucose?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** Completely inhibits liver glucose production
- **B.** Promotes permanent urinary glucose loss
- **C.** Stimulates hepatic glycogen breakdown and gluconeogenesis
- **D.** Stimulates glucose uptake through GLUT4 as its main effect

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: C. Stimulates hepatic glycogen breakdown and gluconeogenesis**

Fasting mein glucagon liver ko stored glycogen todne aur naya glucose banane ka signal de sakta hai. Isse blood glucose support hota hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** GLUT4 uptake insulin se stimulated hota hai. Glucagon liver output badha sakta hai. Urinary glucose loss main function nahi hai.

</details>

### Q17. Which receptor location is typical for many steroid hormones such as cortisol?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** Intracellular cytoplasmic or nuclear receptors

- **C.** Only receptors in the intestinal lumen
- **D.** No receptor is involved

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

</details>

### Q18. Why can a peptide hormone such as insulin signal without entering the target cell?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** It permanently dissolves the plasma membrane
- **B.** It enters only through nuclear pores from outside the cell
- **C.** It acts without any receptor
- **D.** It binds to a membrane receptor that initiates intracellular signaling

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. It binds to a membrane receptor that initiates intracellular signaling**

Insulin cell membrane receptor se bind karti hai. Receptor signal pathways activate karta hai, jo glucose transport aur metabolism affect karti hain.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Membrane dissolve nahi hoti. Nuclear pores extracellular insulin ka direct route nahi hain. Receptor-mediated signaling hoti hai.

</details>

### Q20. A person has reduced plasma osmolality after drinking a large quantity of water. Which normal endocrine response helps restore balance?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** Increased insulin as the main osmolality response
- **B.** Reduced ADH secretion with increased water excretion
- **C.** Marked increased ADH in every case
- **D.** Complete inhibition of kidney filtration

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Reduced ADH secretion with increased water excretion**

Blood dilute hone par hypothalamic osmoreceptors ADH release kam karne mein help karte hain. Kidney zyada dilute urine nikaal sakti hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** High ADH water retain karta hai. Filtration necessarily stop nahi hoti. Insulin primary water-balance hormone nahi hai.

</details>

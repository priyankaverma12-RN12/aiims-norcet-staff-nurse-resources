### Q1. Which alveolar cells form most of the thin surface for gas exchange?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** Medium

- **A.** Type I pneumocytes
- **B.** Type II pneumocytes alone
- **C.** Goblet cells
- **D.** Ciliated columnar cells

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: A. Type I pneumocytes**

Type I pneumocytes flat aur thin hoti hain. Inka thin barrier alveoli aur capillary blood ke beech gas diffusion mein help karta hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Type II cells surfactant banati hain. Goblet cells mucus aur ciliated cells airway clearance mein role rakhti hain.

</details>

### Q2. Why is pulmonary surfactant important at low lung volumes?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** It increases surface tension without limit
- **B.** It blocks all oxygen diffusion
- **C.** It replaces the need for respiratory muscles
- **D.** It lowers alveolar surface tension and helps prevent collapse

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. It lowers alveolar surface tension and helps prevent collapse**

Alveoli ke wet surface par surface tension collapse ki tendency banati hai. Surfactant tension kam karke small alveoli ko stable rakhne mein help karta hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Increased tension collapse risk badhati hai. Oxygen diffusion continue hoti hai. Breathing muscles phir bhi zaroori hain.

</details>

### Q3. Which nerve provides the main motor supply to the diaphragm?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** Medium

- **A.** Facial nerve
- **B.** Hypoglossal nerve
- **C.** Phrenic nerve
- **D.** Vagus nerve

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: C. Phrenic nerve**

Phrenic nerve C3–C5 spinal roots se aati hai aur diaphragm contraction control karti hai. Injury breathing ko affect kar sakti hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Vagus autonomic aur laryngeal functions mein role rakhti hai. Facial face aur hypoglossal tongue muscles control karti hain.

</details>

### Q4. During quiet inspiration, how does alveolar pressure change relative to atmospheric pressure?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** It becomes identical to pulmonary arterial pressure
- **B.** It falls slightly below atmospheric pressure
- **C.** It rises substantially above atmospheric pressure
- **D.** It remains permanently equal despite airflow

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. It falls slightly below atmospheric pressure**

Diaphragm contract hone par thoracic volume badhta hai. Alveolar pressure thoda kam hota hai aur air lungs ke andar flow karti hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Pressure above atmosphere expiration favor karta hai. Airflow ke liye gradient chahiye. Pulmonary arterial pressure blood pressure hai.

</details>

### Q5. Why is quiet expiration normally passive in a healthy adult?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** Medium

- **A.** Alveolar pressure remains below atmosphere during outward airflow
- **B.** Elastic recoil of lungs and chest wall helps expel air
- **C.** The diaphragm actively contracts downward throughout expiration
- **D.** Abdominal muscles must contract maximally with every normal breath

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Elastic recoil of lungs and chest wall helps expel air**

Inspiration ke baad muscles relax karti hain. Lungs elastic recoil se smaller volume ki taraf jaate hain aur air bahar nikalti hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Diaphragm expiration mein generally relax hoti hai. Forced expiration muscles use kar sakti hai. Outward airflow ke liye alveolar pressure slightly higher hota hai.

</details>

### Q6. A patient breathes 14 times/min with a tidal volume of 450 mL. What is minute ventilation?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** 6.3 L/min
- **B.** 3.15 L/min
- **C.** 0.63 L/min
- **D.** 63 L/min

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: A. 6.3 L/min**

Minute ventilation ek minute mein total inspired ya expired air hai. 450 x 14 = 6300 mL/min, yani 6.3 L/min.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** 3.15 half result hai. 0.63 aur 63 conversion errors hain.

</details>

### Q7. A patient has tidal volume 500 mL, dead space 150 mL and respiratory rate 16/min. What is alveolar ventilation?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** 8.0 L/min
- **B.** 2.4 L/min
- **C.** 10.4 L/min
- **D.** 5.6 L/min

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. 5.6 L/min**

Har breath mein 500 minus 150 = 350 mL gas exchange region tak jaati hai. 350 x 16 = 5600 mL/min.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** 8 total minute ventilation hai. 2.4 dead-space ventilation hai. 10.4 dead space ko galat add karta hai.

</details>

### Q8. Which lung volume cannot be measured directly with ordinary spirometry?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** Inspiratory reserve volume
- **B.** Expiratory reserve volume
- **C.** Residual volume
- **D.** Tidal volume

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: C. Residual volume**

Residual volume maximal expiration ke baad bhi lungs mein reh jaati hai. Ye air spirometer mein exhale nahi hoti, isliye direct measure nahi ki ja sakti.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Tidal, inspiratory reserve aur expiratory reserve volumes spirometry se estimate ho sakte hain.

</details>

### Q9. Vital capacity is 4.0 L and residual volume is 1.2 L. What is total lung capacity?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** 4.0 L
- **B.** 1.2 L
- **C.** 5.2 L
- **D.** 2.8 L

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: C. 5.2 L**

Total lung capacity maximal inspiration par lungs mein total air hai. Vital capacity plus residual volume = 4.0 + 1.2 = 5.2 L.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** 2.8 subtraction hai. 4 vital capacity hai. 1.2 residual volume hai.

</details>

### Q10. Which structure is the usual site of gas exchange between alveolar air and pulmonary blood?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** Medium

- **A.** Vocal cords
- **B.** Respiratory membrane
- **C.** Tracheal cartilage
- **D.** Main bronchial smooth muscle

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Respiratory membrane**

Respiratory membrane alveolar epithelium aur capillary endothelium ka thin barrier hai. Oxygen aur CO2 partial pressure gradients ke saath cross karti hain.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Trachea aur main bronchi air conduct karte hain. Vocal cords sound aur airway protection mein role rakhti hain.

</details>

### Q11. A pulmonary capillary receives blood but its adjacent alveolus is completely unventilated. Which physiological pattern is present?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** Shunt-like perfusion with very low ventilation-perfusion ratio
- **B.** High ventilation with absent perfusion
- **C.** Normal ventilation-perfusion matching
- **D.** Increased anatomical dead space as the sole mechanism

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: A. Shunt-like perfusion with very low ventilation-perfusion ratio**

Blood aise alveolus se pass ho raha hai jahan fresh air nahi pahunch rahi. Isliye V/Q ratio low ya zero hota hai aur oxygenation compromise ho sakti hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Absent perfusion with ventilation dead-space pattern hai. Matching normal nahi hai. Anatomical dead space conducting airways se related hai.

</details>

### Q12. An alveolus is ventilated normally but its capillary blood supply is completely obstructed. What is the physiological consequence?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** A pure right-to-left shunt occurs at that alveolus
- **B.** Gas exchange becomes more efficient
- **C.** The alveolus stops containing air in every case
- **D.** Alveolar dead space increases

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. Alveolar dead space increases**

Air alveolus tak pahunch rahi hai lekin blood gas exchange ke liye present nahi hai. Ye wasted ventilation ya alveolar dead space hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Shunt mein blood flow hota hai par ventilation absent hoti hai. Exchange improve nahi hota. Air still present ho sakti hai.

</details>

### Q13. Which condition shifts the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve to the right?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** Decreased temperature and decreased carbon dioxide
- **B.** Increased blood pH alone
- **C.** Reduced 2,3-BPG
- **D.** Increased temperature and increased carbon dioxide

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. Increased temperature and increased carbon dioxide**

Active tissues mein CO2 aur temperature badh sakte hain. Hemoglobin oxygen ko aasani se release karti hai, jise right shift kehte hain.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Low temperature, low CO2, higher pH aur reduced 2,3-BPG generally left shift favor karte hain.

</details>

### Q14. In which form is most carbon dioxide transported in normal blood?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** Medium

- **A.** Oxygen-bound hemoglobin
- **B.** Carbon dioxide gas bubbles
- **C.** Bicarbonate ions
- **D.** Physically dissolved gas only

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: C. Bicarbonate ions**

RBCs mein carbonic anhydrase CO2 aur water ka reaction fast karti hai. Bicarbonate ban kar plasma mein transport hota hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Dissolved CO2 aur carbamino compounds smaller fractions hain. Oxygen binding CO2 ka major transport form nahi hai. Gas bubbles normal transport nahi hain.

</details>

### Q15. What is the main stimulus for central chemoreceptor-mediated changes in ventilation in most healthy adults?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** Changes in skin temperature alone
- **B.** Changes in cerebrospinal fluid pH related to arterial CO2
- **C.** Direct detection of every plasma sodium fluctuation
- **D.** Direct sensing of oxygen by the diaphragm

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Changes in cerebrospinal fluid pH related to arterial CO2**

CO2 blood-brain barrier cross karke CSF mein hydrogen ions badha sakti hai. Central chemoreceptors is pH change ko sense karke ventilation adjust karte hain.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Sodium primary stimulus nahi hai. Diaphragm chemoreceptor nahi hai. Skin temperature alone central response explain nahi karti.

</details>

### Q17. Why is an aspirated foreign body more likely to enter the right main bronchus in many adults?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** The right main bronchus is wider, shorter and more vertical
- **B.** The left bronchus always has no lumen
- **C.** The right bronchus carries blood instead of air
- **D.** The right lung lacks alveoli

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

</details>

### Q19. Why does loss of normal negative intrapleural pressure during pneumothorax permit lung collapse?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** Pleural fluid turns into bone
- **B.** The diaphragm permanently contracts without relaxation
- **C.** The pressure gradient helping keep lung expanded is reduced
- **D.** Alveoli immediately stop producing all gases

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: C. The pressure gradient helping keep lung expanded is reduced**

Normal pleural pressure lung ke outward expansion ko support karta hai. Pleural space mein air enter ho toh transpulmonary pressure kam ho sakta hai aur elastic recoil lung collapse kar sakta hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** Gas production mechanism nahi hai. Pleural fluid bone nahi banta. Permanent diaphragm contraction required nahi hai.

</details>

### Q20. Which statement about pulmonary compliance is correct?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High

- **A.** Compliance is independent of pressure and volume
- **B.** A stiff lung requires a larger pressure change for the same volume change
- **C.** High compliance always means the lung is difficult to inflate
- **D.** Compliance measures only oxygen saturation

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. A stiff lung requires a larger pressure change for the same volume change**

Compliance volume change divided by pressure change hai. Stiff lung ki compliance low hoti hai, isliye inflation ke liye zyada pressure lagta hai.

**Baaki options kyun sahi nahi hain?** High compliance mein inflation relatively easy hoti hai. Saturation oxygen binding measure karti hai. Compliance pressure-volume relation hi hai.

</details>

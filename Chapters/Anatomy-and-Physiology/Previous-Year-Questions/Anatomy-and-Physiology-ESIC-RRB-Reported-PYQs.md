### PYQ1. What anatomical term denotes a hole in a bone that can transmit a nerve or blood vessel?

**Reported exam:** ESIC Nursing Officer, 2016 Shift 1

[Question source](https://nprep.in/question-answer/an-opening-in-the-bone-through-which-blood-vessels-nerves-and-ligaments-7OQCTEdyxSiYfDEOIKPrO)

- **A.** Foramen
- **B.** Fossa
- **C.** Crest
- **D.** Tubercle

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: A. Foramen**

Foramen bone ki opening hai. Fossa depression, crest raised ridge aur tubercle small rounded projection hota hai. In landmarks se nerves aur vessels ki location samajh aati hai.

</details>

### PYQ2. A skeletal muscle fascicle is a bundle of which structural elements?

**Reported exam:** RRB Staff Nurse Mumbai, 2015

[Question source](https://nprep.in/question-answer/skeletal-muscles-are-composed-of-a-large-number-of-O6TM931Txd0xv-QhaTRON)

- **A.** Osteocytes
- **B.** Nephrons
- **C.** Alveolar sacs
- **D.** Skeletal muscle fibers

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. Skeletal muscle fibers**

Fascicle multiple muscle fibers ka bundle hai. Muscle fiber ek elongated contractile cell hoti hai. Osteocytes bone cells, nephrons renal units aur alveolar sacs respiratory structures hain.

</details>

### PYQ3. Which broad functional groups commonly correspond to the anterior and posterior forearm compartments?

**Reported exam:** RRB Staff Nurse Mumbai, 2015

[Question source](https://nprep.in/question-answer/muscles-of-the-forearm-are-divided-into-which-group-hMiOcpPjcdsapEft8_YSF)

- **A.** Only respiratory and cardiac muscle groups
- **B.** Quadriceps and hamstrings
- **C.** Flexor-pronator and extensor-supinator groups
- **D.** Only hip abductors and adductors

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: C. Flexor-pronator and extensor-supinator groups**

Anterior forearm mainly flexor-pronator aur posterior mainly extensor-supinator muscles contain karta hai. Hip groups aur quadriceps-hamstrings lower limb mein hain. Respiratory-cardiac groups is regional classification ka part nahi hain.

</details>

### PYQ4. Which connection is classically formed by a ligament at a joint?

**Reported exam:** RRB Staff Nurse Jaipur, 6 June 2018

[Question source](https://nprep.in/question-answer/the-ligaments-join-gXWtD9ilFPLLYK5TiOPTB)

- **A.** Artery to vein
- **B.** Bone to bone
- **C.** Muscle to bone
- **D.** Neuron to muscle fiber

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Bone to bone**

Ligament bones connect karke joint stability support karta hai. Tendon muscle-bone connect karta hai. Neuromuscular junction nerve-muscle contact hai. Artery-vein connection vascular system ka concept hai.

</details>

### PYQ5. The prominent anterior part of the larynx known as Adam's apple belongs to which cartilage?

**Reported exam:** ESIC Nursing Officer, 2019 Shift 1

[Question source](https://nprep.in/question-answer/the-adam-s-apple-is-formed-by-5ruFFmE856iL1BRnQCHRb)

- **A.** Arytenoid cartilage
- **B.** Thyroid cartilage
- **C.** Cricoid cartilage
- **D.** Epiglottis

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Thyroid cartilage**

Thyroid cartilage ke anterior plates laryngeal prominence banate hain. Cricoid lower complete ring hai. Epiglottis airway protection mein aur arytenoids vocal fold movements mein role rakhte hain.

</details>

### PYQ8. Which muscle provides the main force for quiet inspiration?

**Reported exam:** ESIC Nursing Officer, 2016 Shift 2

[Question source](https://nprep.in/question-answer/muscle-of-inspiration-is-nbMkgo1phs5OO7MTF_CTW)

- **A.** Internal intercostals during forced expiration
- **B.** External anal sphincter
- **C.** Diaphragm
- **D.** Rectus abdominis

<details><summary>Answer aur explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: C. Diaphragm**

Diaphragm contract hokar flatten hoti hai aur thoracic volume badhati hai. Abdominal aur internal intercostal muscles forced expiration mein assist kar sakti hain. Anal sphincter continence se related hai.

</details>

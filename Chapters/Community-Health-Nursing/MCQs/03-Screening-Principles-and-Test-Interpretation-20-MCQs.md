### Q1. Which term describes the proportion of truly diseased persons who test positive?

**Difficulty:** Easy | **Priority:** Medium | **Topic:** Screening Principles and Test Interpretation | **Type:** Original practice

- **A.** Positive predictive value
- **B.** Negative predictive value
- **C.** Sensitivity
- **D.** Specificity

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: C. Sensitivity**

- **A.** Positive predictive value positive results mein actual disease dekhti hai.
- **B.** Negative predictive value negative results mein disease-free status dekhti hai.
- **C.** Sensitivity mein actual disease wale logon mein se positive test kitne hain, ye measure hota hai.
- **D.** Specificity actual disease-free logon mein negative result ka proportion hai.

**Exam clue:** Sensitivity ka denominator diseased population hai.

</details>

### Q2. A test correctly identifies 180 of 200 disease-free people as negative. What is its specificity?

**Difficulty:** Easy | **Priority:** Medium | **Topic:** Screening Principles and Test Interpretation | **Type:** Original practice

- **A.** 10%
- **B.** 80%
- **C.** 100%
- **D.** 90%

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. 90%**

- **A.** 10% false positive proportion hai.
- **B.** 80% diye gaye true negatives aur total ka ratio nahi hai.
- **C.** 100% ke liye sabhi 200 disease-free log negative hone chahiye.
- **D.** 180 true negatives ko 200 disease-free logon se divide karne par 90% milta hai.

**Exam clue:** Specificity = TN/(TN+FP).

</details>

### Q3. Of 80 truly diseased people, 68 screen positive. What is the false-negative proportion among the diseased group?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Screening Principles and Test Interpretation | **Type:** Original practice

- **A.** 15%
- **B.** 85%
- **C.** 12%
- **D.** 20%

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: A. 15%**

- **A.** 80 mein 68 positive hain, toh 12 false negatives hain. 12/80 se 15% aata hai.
- **B.** 85% sensitivity hai, false-negative proportion nahi.
- **C.** 12 false negatives ki count hai, percentage nahi.
- **D.** 20% ke liye 16 false negatives chahiye.

**Exam clue:** False-negative proportion is 1 minus sensitivity.

</details>

### Q4. A screening test produces 45 true positives and 15 false positives. What is its positive predictive value?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Screening Principles and Test Interpretation | **Type:** Original practice

- **A.** 60%
- **B.** 75%
- **C.** 25%
- **D.** 33.3%

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. 75%**

- **A.** 60 positive results ki count hai, percentage nahi.
- **B.** Total positive 60 hain aur 45 actual disease wale hain, isliye PPV 75% hai.
- **C.** 25% positive results mein false positives ka proportion hai.
- **D.** 33.3% false positives ko true positives se divide karta hai, total positives se nahi.

**Exam clue:** PPV ka denominator sab positive tests hai.

</details>

### Q5. A test produces 190 true negatives and 10 false negatives. What is the negative predictive value?

**Difficulty:** Medium | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Screening Principles and Test Interpretation | **Type:** Original practice

- **A.** 90%
- **B.** 19%
- **C.** 95%
- **D.** 5%

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: C. 95%**

- **A.** 90% is data se calculated NPV nahi hai.
- **B.** 19 true negatives aur false negatives ka ratio hai, percentage nahi.
- **C.** Negative results total 200 hain aur 190 disease-free hain, isliye NPV 95% hai.
- **D.** 5% negative tests mein missed disease ka proportion hai.

**Exam clue:** NPV = TN/(TN+FN).

</details>

### Q6. The same test with unchanged sensitivity and specificity is used in a population with substantially lower disease prevalence. What usually happens?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Screening Principles and Test Interpretation | **Type:** Original practice

- **A.** Positive predictive value necessarily increases
- **B.** Sensitivity automatically becomes zero
- **C.** Specificity automatically becomes 100%
- **D.** Positive predictive value decreases

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. Positive predictive value decreases**

- **A.** Low prevalence usually PPV ko kam karti hai, badhati nahi.
- **B.** Question mein sensitivity unchanged di gayi hai.
- **C.** Specificity bhi unchanged hai, prevalence change se perfect nahi ban jaati.
- **D.** Low prevalence mein disease-free people ka proportion zyada hota hai, isliye positives mein false positives ka relative share badh sakta hai.

**Exam clue:** Predictive values prevalence se depend karte hain.

</details>

### Q7. A laboratory lowers the threshold above which a continuous screening test is considered positive. What is the usual trade-off?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Screening Principles and Test Interpretation | **Type:** Original practice

- **A.** Sensitivity increases while specificity decreases
- **B.** Sensitivity decreases while specificity increases
- **C.** Both become perfect automatically
- **D.** Both remain unchanged for every possible threshold

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: A. Sensitivity increases while specificity decreases**

- **A.** Lower threshold par more diseased people positive mil sakte hain, lekin more disease-free people bhi positive ho sakte hain.
- **B.** Higher threshold par generally opposite trade-off hota hai.
- **C.** Threshold change se imperfect test automatically perfect nahi hota.
- **D.** Classification boundary change hone se test performance measures change ho sakte hain.

**Exam clue:** Lower positive threshold usually catches more cases and more false positives.

</details>

### Q8. Two screening tests are interpreted in parallel, with a person positive if either test is positive. What is the usual purpose?

- **B.** Increase combined sensitivity
- **C.** Increase combined specificity at all costs
- **D.** Eliminate every false-positive result

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Increase combined sensitivity**

- **A.** Early disease detect karna sensitivity badhane ka reason ho sakta hai.
- **B.** Either test positive rule missed disease kam karne mein help kar sakta hai.
- **C.** Parallel rule se combined specificity usually kam ho sakti hai.
- **D.** More positive classifications se false positives eliminate nahi hote.

**Exam clue:** Parallel testing favors sensitivity.

</details>

### Q9. Two tests are interpreted in series, with a final positive result only if both are positive. Which performance measure is usually favored?

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

</details>

### Q10. A screening programme reports longer survival after diagnosis but no reduction in the time of death. Which bias may explain the apparent benefit?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Screening Principles and Test Interpretation | **Type:** Original practice

- **A.** Recall bias
- **B.** Confounding by smoking necessarily
- **C.** Randomization failure necessarily
- **D.** Lead-time bias

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: D. Lead-time bias**

- **A.** Recall bias past events yaad karne ki accuracy se related hai.
- **B.** Smoking ka role question mein establish nahi hua.
- **C.** Randomization ki failure ke bina bhi early diagnosis survival ko artificially extend dikha sakta hai.
- **D.** Early diagnosis se diagnosis-to-death interval bada dikh sakta hai, even jab death ka time same rahe.

**Exam clue:** Survival since diagnosis alone screening benefit prove nahi karta.

</details>

### Q11. Screening preferentially detects slowly progressing tumors because they remain detectable for a longer preclinical period. Which bias is this?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Screening Principles and Test Interpretation | **Type:** Original practice

- **A.** Length-time bias
- **B.** Observer blinding
- **C.** Selection by random allocation
- **D.** Attrition due to treatment withdrawal

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: A. Length-time bias**

- **A.** Slow progression wale tumors screening window mein zyada time rehte hain, isliye unka detection disproportionately zyada ho sakta hai.
- **B.** Blinding observer ko group information se protect karta hai, disease duration pattern ko define nahi karta.
- **C.** Random allocation intervention studies ki technique hai.
- **D.** Attrition follow-up loss hai, slow disease detection ka mechanism nahi.

**Exam clue:** Length bias favors detection of slow-progressing disease.

</details>

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

### Q20. Which outcome provides stronger evidence of a beneficial cancer screening programme than an increase in the number of early-stage diagnoses alone?

**Difficulty:** Difficult | **Priority:** High | **Topic:** Screening Principles and Test Interpretation | **Type:** Original practice

- **A.** Increase in detection of abnormalities that would never become symptomatic
- **B.** Reduction in relevant disease-specific mortality with harms assessed in an appropriate comparative evaluation

- **D.** Increase in five-year survival after diagnosis alone

<details><summary>Answer aur easy explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Reduction in relevant disease-specific mortality with harms assessed in an appropriate comparative evaluation**

- **A.** Overdiagnosis unnecessary harm create kar sakti hai aur detection count ko inflate karti hai.
- **B.** Appropriate comparison mein mortality reduction aur harms dono evaluate karne se true net benefit ka better evidence milta hai.
- **C.** Test volume benefit ka outcome measure nahi hai.
- **D.** Lead-time bias survival duration ko misleadingly bada dikha sakti hai.

**Exam clue:** Evaluate patient-important outcomes and harms, not detection counts alone.

</details>

### Q1. When a chapati cooks on a hot tawa, which mechanism primarily transfers heat at the surface touching the tawa?

**Difficulty:** 🟢 Easy | **Priority:** 🟠 HIGH YIELD | **Topic:** Conduction | **Type:** Original practice

- **A.** Convection of air alone
- **B.** Conduction
- **C.** Evaporative cooling
- **D.** Microwave heating

<details><summary>Answer aur detailed explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Conduction**

- **A. Incorrect:** Convection mein liquid ya gas ka movement heat carry karta hai. Tawa se touching surface par primary transfer direct contact hai.

- **B. Correct:** Tawa aur chapati ke contact surface par heat direct transfer hoti hai. Is direct thermal contact ko conduction kehte hain.

- **C. Incorrect:** Evaporation moisture remove karke cooling kar sakti hai. Yeh hot metal se heat transfer ka mechanism nahi hai.

- **D. Incorrect:** Microwave heating electromagnetic energy use karti hai. Ordinary hot tawa is scenario mein microwave source nahi hai.

**Exam clue:** Direct contact means conduction.

- **C.** All vitamins become heat-proof after germination
- **D.** Germination makes subsequent food-safety precautions unnecessary

<details><summary>Answer aur detailed explanation dekho</summary>

**Correct: B. Germination can improve digestibility and the bioavailability of some nutrients**

- **A. Incorrect:** Growing sprout stored reserves use karta hai. Unlimited energy creation biological process nahi hai.

- **B. Correct:** Germination during suitable processing biochemical changes la sakti hai jo digestibility aur kuch micronutrients ki availability improve karti hain. Benefit food aur preparation par depend karta hai.

- **C. Incorrect:** Germination se vitamins universally heat stable nahi ho jaate. Later preparation conditions nutrient retention affect kar sakti hain.

- **D. Incorrect:** Sprouting conditions microbial growth bhi support kar sakti hain. Hygiene aur suitable safe cooking precautions ab bhi important hain, especially vulnerable patients ke liye.

**Exam clue:** Germination can improve nutrient availability but does not guarantee food safety.
